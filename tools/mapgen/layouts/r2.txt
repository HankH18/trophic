# AUTHORED LAYOUT for "r2" — Brackmarsh. Hand-designed, not seeded output.
#
# The tile composer stamps one tile per character and collision is normalised from the same
# characters, so painting and collision correspond by construction and THIS FILE IS THE MAP.
# Re-running tools/mapgen/seed.ts over it would overwrite a design, not a draft.
#
# ── coordinate space ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
# space        map-native pixels, origin TOP-LEFT (data/nodes/*.json props[].x/y are in this space)
# grid         64 cols × 64 rows, one character per collision cell
# cell         16 native px  (balance.map.collisionCellPx; 1024/64 = 16)
# map          1024 × 1024 native px, rendered ×2 → 2048 world px
# tiles        64 × 64 movement tiles of 32 world px
# col ← x      col = floor(nativeX / 16);  a cell's centre is native (16·col + 8, 16·row + 8)
# centre       native (512, 512) = cell (32, 32) = the tile firstWalkableTile starts on
#
# ── authored from ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
# biome        Marsh (data/nodes/r2.json)
# adjacency    brackwater = BOTTOM edge, bowerdeep = TOP edge. Two mouths, no side mouths.
# subject      tools/comfy/manifest.json _nodes.r2 — "mostly dark open water: wide brackish marsh
#              of black shallow water, brown peat banks, reed clumps and half-sunken dead trees…
#              One narrow weathered plank boardwalk winds the length of the picture in wide
#              shallow S-bends, deep channel water on its left and open shallows on its right,
#              crossing the exact middle of the picture on bare planking with nothing on it,
#              dropping at the bottom onto a mud landing of rotting mooring posts and a leaning
#              jetty toward the stilt town, and reaching firm peat at the top toward the forest."
# shipped line amb.r2-ferryhand.base — "Channel's on your left going out. Shallows on your right."
#              The asymmetry is unconditional, so every row of this grid obeys it: deep water WEST
#              of the planks, wadeable shallows EAST of them, the whole length of the map.
# settlement   no — zero building footprints, and therefore no doors (checkSettlementOnly)
# props        r2-antler-causeway @ (268,402) → cell (16,25); r2-idol-shallows @ (664,612) → cell (41,38)
#              Both sit where data/nodes/r2.json puts them. Neither had to move.
#
# ── the design, and why the walk bends where it does ─────────────────────────
# · ONE boardwalk (:) three cells wide runs mouth to mouth in two wide shallow bends. It is the
#   only built thing on the route and the only way across without wading.
# · The water DEEPENS from north to south, because Brackwater is a stilt town on the water and
#   Bowerdeep is forest: a creek head two cells wide under the treeline at the top, a ferry
#   channel fifteen wide at the bottom. That gradient is why no two screens of this map repeat.
# · FIRST BEND, west (rows 16–33): a peat shelf — the causeway — is the one piece of firm ground
#   on the channel side, and the walk climbs onto it rather than staying out on piles. The
#   leaning antler stone stands at the shelf's western lip, cell (16,25), where the peat drops
#   into the channel: "leans downhill toward the water", with the shelf banked against its
#   uphill side. Eight cells of peat separate it from the planks — a short walk off the route,
#   which is the only reason to leave the boardwalk on the deep side.
# · SECOND BEND, east (rows 36–52): the channel opens out below the middle, so the walk is
#   pushed east onto the shallow flats. The sunken idol stands out in the shallows at (41,38),
#   five cells east of the planks, in a clearing the reed beds leave around it — "where the
#   reeds thin, turned back to face the town".
# · CENTRE (32,32) is bare planking with nothing on it, as the subject line requires. The
#   channel runs along its west side and open shallows along its east: the middle of the map is
#   the moment the asymmetry is most legible.
# · BOTTOM: the planks drop onto a mud landing (rows 55–62), rotting mooring posts (F) standing
#   off both lips of it, and the jetty carries the walk out to the bottom edge between its two
#   rails — so the mouth toward Brackwater is a place, not a line leaving a blank frame.
# · TOP: firm brown peat under the forest edge of Bowerdeep, the treeline closing the frame with
#   the mouth cut through it.
# · NO BORDER RING. The frame is landscape: a drowned wood of dead-tree stands cut by black
#   water on the west, open water and standing snags on the east, forest at the top, the ferry
#   channel at the bottom. Blocked-margin depth runs 3–38 cells and is never uniform.
#
# ── encounter terrain (the "," cells) ────────────────────────────────────────
# · 408 of 1657 walkable cells = 24.6%, against data/balance.json encounterTileFraction 0.25.
# · 14 patches, largest 110 cells, smallest 7: reed beds ON the peat banks (a player reads "reeds grow where
#   the ground is firm"), plus reed clumps standing in the open shallows.
# · None within four cells of (32,32): the arrival apron is bare on every entry.
# · The boardwalk itself carries no encounter cell and is kept two cells clear of them, so a
#   player who stays on the planks crosses the whole route without a forced encounter — verified
#   bottom mouth to top mouth. Everything worth catching is off the planks, in the reeds.
# · NOTE: the engine does not gate encounters on these cells yet (encounterTileFraction is a
#   scalar and rolls uniformly). Deriving the mask from this grid is a code-lane ticket; the art
#   leads here deliberately.
#
# ── anchors: what the composer must stamp UNDER them ─────────────────────────
# Anchors are documentary and flatten to terrain. Where they sit is not arbitrary:
#   @ (32,32)  boardwalk plank — ":" . The centre must stay bare planking.
#   ^ (33, 0)  boardwalk plank — ":" , the mouth onward to bowerdeep.
#   ^ (31,63)  jetty plank     — ":" , the mouth back to brackwater.
#   * (16,25)  peat bank       — "." , the antler stone's shelf.
#   * (41,38)  shallow water   — "~" , the idol stands in the water, not on a bank.
#
# ── verified (tools/mapgen/verify.ts semantics, re-run over this body) ───────
# edge runs      top [(32,34)] · bottom [(30,32)] · left [] · right [] — sides sealed, one mouth each
# arrival        (32,32) walkable, nothing built and no encounter cell within three cells of it
# reachability   1657 walkable cells, 1657 reachable from the arrival tile, zero marooned
# anchors        every mouth and both props reachable; no doors exist to be unreachable
# paths          one ":" component of 205 cells, containing BOTH mouths; no path ends in open ground
# frame          border ring uses ":" "F" "T" "W" "^" — not a uniform one-cell wall
#
# ── decisions this file made ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
# · THE PLACE. A brackish marsh crossed on one plank boardwalk, with the deep channel on the
#   walk's left going out and the wadeable shallows on its right for the whole length of the
#   map — that split is shipped dialogue (amb.r2-ferryhand.base), not a stylistic choice, and
#   nothing else in the region can borrow it without contradicting a line the game speaks.
# · THE ARRIVAL APRON IS CLEAR. Seven cells of deep channel used to sit inside the 7x7 box
#   around (32,32) — open water two rows from the tile the player lands on at every entry.
#   The channel is pulled back and its mass carried further west instead.
# · THE JETTY AND THE BOTTOM MOUTH MOVED TO COL 38, to sit exactly on brackwater's top mouth;
#   the seam used to snap thirteen columns sideways. The jetty now runs out over the shallows
#   from the east side of the mud landing, between its rails, which is what a jetty does.
# · ONE BOARDWALK, three planks wide, one connected run containing both mouths, no spur.
#   The antler stone and the idol are reached by walking off the planks, never by a spur.
# · ENCOUNTER TERRAIN. 24.7% of walkable, on the peat banks and in the reed clumps, none of it
#   on the boardwalk, so the route can be crossed without a forced roll.
#
# ── legend (tools/mapgen/legend.ts) ──────────────────────────────────────────
#   .  floor  open-ground         plain walkable ground; the default fill
#   ,  floor  scrub               walkable but textured — long grass, ferns, heather
#   :  floor  path-corridor       the worn track that joins the map edges through the middle
#   o  floor  centre-clearing     the open region at native (512,512) the spawn search lands in
#   ~  floor  shallow             walkable water margin — wadeable, not a wall
#   #  WALL   wall                generic impassable mass with no more specific kind
#   B  WALL   building-footprint  a building the player walks around, never through
#   T  WALL   tree-mass           trunks and thicket dense enough to stop a walk
#   W  WALL   water               open water — deep enough to block
#   R  WALL   rock                basalt, scree, standing stone, crater rim
#   F  WALL   fence               a thin built barrier — pen rail, jetty edge, ore-cart rail
#   *  ANCH   prop-anchor         a data/nodes/*.json props[] entry, at floor(x/cellPx), floor(y/cellPx)
#   @  ANCH   spawn-anchor        documentary marker for the centre cell the spawn search starts from
#   ^  ANCH   edge-mouth          the cell where a corridor meets the map edge; one per declared adjacency
#   D  ANCH   door-anchor         the walkable cell a building is entered from
#
# In this map: "." is brown peat bank, "," is reed bed, ":" is weathered plank, "~" is black
# shallow water, "W" is the deep channel, "T" is half-sunken dead trees and the forest edge,
# "F" is mooring posts and jetty rail. "o", "B", "R" and "D" are unused — no clearing, no
# building, no rock, no door on this route.
#
# Only "#"-class characters block. tools/mapgen/layout.ts normalises every WALL character to "#"
# before game/collision.ts gridFromAscii sees it; ANCH characters parse as floor and are read again
# on a second pass. Region kinds are open strings — add a row to the legend, not a case to a switch.
