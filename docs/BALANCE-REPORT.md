## 1. The reasonable player — stated, not assumed

A harness whose player model is a black box tunes toward nonsense, so here is the whole model.
The authoritative copy is the header of `tools/harness/player.ts`; nothing in it is an invented
number — every clause reads from `data/balance.json`, `data/gyms.json`, `data/nodes/*.json` or
`data/species.json`.

1. **Where they have been.** At gym *Y*: the towns of gyms 1..*Y* plus every node adjacent to them
   (`gyms.json.town` + `nodes/*.json.adjacency`). Nothing from a later act.
2. **What they could own.** Every line with a live population at one of those nodes, minus the
   sign-chain-gated ones (`balance.encounter.signChainGatedHabitatTag`) and the Ancients, plus the
   starter lines — identified structurally as the lines that appear in no node's populations.
   At gym 1 that pool is 9 lines wide.
3. **Team size.** The leader's roster size, capped at `balance.party.maxPartySize` (6) —
   so 4/4/5/6/6/6. One slot is always a starter (bible §8: Maren hands you one).
4. **Level.** Two models, both reported: **TRAINED** = the leader's rank-and-file level
   (`max` over `gym.slots`), i.e. parity with the team and two under the signature; **UNTRAINED** =
   `balance.encounter.levelByYear[Y-1]` (6/12/18/24/30/36), the level the region's own
   wild population sits at. On shipped data UNTRAINED is exactly four levels below TRAINED.
5. **Age.** Drawn from `balance.encounter.ageWeights` (J 0.5 / A 0.4 /
   E 0.1) — the shipped wild distribution. The team came out of the grass, so it is
   aged like the grass. **This is not a modelling liberty: aging happens at year turns and the year
   turn fires ON gym victory, so a year-*Y* team really is whatever year *Y*'s grass produced.**
6. **Traits.** `balance.traits.slotsPerWildCreature` (1) per creature from
   `balance.traits.pool`; Elders roll the rare pool at `traits.elderRareChance` (0.25),
   everyone else rolls common — including the five *negative* common traits.
7. **Which team they field.** 8 candidate teams are drafted; the best is kept, first by
   super-effective coverage of the leader's roster, then by total `effectiveStats`. A player who
   thought about it for a minute — not one who solved it.
8. **How they play.** Move choice is the **same greedy argmax** `core/battle.ts` gives the leader, so a
   win rate measures the two teams and not a gap between two AIs. They never set up (every utility
   and the two 0-power role moves score 0), never switch for advantage (only the forced post-faint
   switch), carry one salve per member and spend one only when the heal exceeds the enemy's expected
   damage that turn. The policy consumes **zero RNG draws**, exactly like the enemy policy.

**What the model does not do, and which way each omission pushes:** no advantage-switching, no
setup, no status fishing, no Lure, no tonics, no relics/boons/inherited trait slots, and a pool that
can hand it a Producer or a Scavenger — all push the win rate **down**. Best-of-8 drafting, a
uniform level across the team, and the player *seat* itself push it **up**. Section 2's controls
price the last one exactly.

## 4. Mode (c) — six years against the ecology

### 4.1 Six years of scripted play

No fight is simulated in this mode, and that is a deduction rather than a shortcut: **KO is a faint**
(Hard Req 8 / bible §13.14 — lifespan is the mortality system), so a battle cannot move a population
by one individual. The only channel from play into the ecology is the catch, and the harness resolves
each catch with the shipped §7.4 `catchChance` against a real `Battler`, using the reference player
already declared in `balance.harness.overcatch` (weighted snare, species bait, thrown at
50% HP). Season boundaries come from `core/clock.ts` and the year turn from
`core/lifecycle.ts`, so the shipped Cullbrand raid fires through the real code path and the
24-season-steps-per-game invariant holds (18 clock boundaries + 6 year turns).

Three profiles, all bounded by shipped constants rather than by an invented rate:

- **baseline** — no player at all. The control: a line that dies here was never anybody's fault.
- **dex** — catches one of every line it meets, then leaves that line alone. At most 27 catches
  over six years. This is *normal play*.
- **collector** — catches everything it can until party and box are full

### 4.3 The over-catch sweep — the beat as a rate, at every node

The fixture is one seed at one node. This is the same protocol at **every node that has a top of
web**, over 128 seeds each, in the two readings and the two shapes that build-doc §10.2 and the
fixture respectively imply:

- **reading `primary`** — force the predator's `primaryPrey` only. §10.2's literal wording.
- **reading `limiting`** — force `limitingPrey`: the minimal set of catchable pools whose loss actually
  starves the predator (DEVIATIONS A2-D10).
- **shape `hold`** — drive each target under `cascade.preyForcedFraction` (10%) at the start of
  every one of `cascade.forcingSeasons` (2) seasons. Maximum forcing.
- **shape `effort`** — remove `opening / forcingSeasons` individuals per forcing season and then stop.
  This is the shape `overcatch-script.json` actually records (7 + 7 out of 14), generalised.

Nothing here is scripted into the sim (G6). The harness issues `applyCatch` — the same call a landed
snare makes — and then lets `core/ecosim.ts` run its own arithmetic.

Columns: **apex → 0** is the first half of the beat. **throne refilled** is something alive sitting at
the top of the node's *live* web afterwards. **and rising** additionally requires that tier to be
strictly above where it stood when the apex hit zero, within `cascade.mesoReleaseSeasons` (4) seasons —
build-doc §10.2's strict clause, and the full never-cut beat.

**Read "throne refilled" carefully.** It asks only that *something* sits at the top of the live web
afterwards, and a Grazer inherits the throne by default once every predator above it is gone. A row
with a refilled throne AND a high co-extinction rate is a two-tier collapse that left the herbivores
in charge, not a mesopredator release. The column that means the demo beat is **and rising**.

## 5. Findings

### MEASURED

- **1 of 6 gyms is inside the §10.5 band** under the TRAINED model (500 seeds each). Misses: Maribel 38.6% (-41.4 pp), Odo 66.0% (-14.0 pp), Vesper 56.8% (-3.2 pp), Sable 59.8% (-0.2 pp), Magnate Greaves 36.2% (-8.8 pp).
- **Four levels are worth +13.0 pp of win rate on average** across the six gyms (TRAINED minus UNTRAINED).
- **The player seat is worth roughly +46.5 pp in a true mirror match** (mean 96.5% over the six leaders' own teams fielded against themselves). Every win rate in §2 already includes it.
- **`encounter.ageWeights` is worth +23.8 pp**: forcing the shipped J/A/E mix to all-Adult moves the mean gym win rate by that much.
- **10 of 24 non-Ancient lines sit outside the 35%–65% band** on the peer reading. Dominant: Bramblore 79%, Mirefin 77%, Gloomoth 73%, Vulkindle 72%, Duskstalk 72%, Nibbin 71%. Weak: Skir 34%, Loamunder 27%, Sporantle 13%, Carricrow 8%.
- **The weakest line in the game is Carricrow at 7.9%** against peers (base 50/60/45/75/70). Its worst matchups: Ashvole 0%, Barkhide 0%, Vulkindle 0%.
- **59.0% of the 702 ordered matchups are decided** — one side wins all 64 seeds. The roster is a hard-counter lattice, not a field of close fights.
- **Every non-Ancient line spends exactly 300 base-stat points**, so the round-robin is measuring an ALLOCATION, not a quality gap. The base-stat total correlates with win rate at r = 0.000, exactly as a fixed budget must.
- **The strongest single predictor of a line's peer win rate is ins, r = -0.582** — and it is **NEGATIVE**. Per stat: ins -0.58, atk 0.34, spd 0.09, def -0.05, hp 0.01. Best composite: Attack 0.343.
- **Instinct is a stat that costs win rate to buy** (r = -0.582, range 30–70 across the roster). bible §2 gives Instinct three jobs — "crit chance, bait success, sign-reading quality" — but `core/battle.ts` reads it for exactly one: `critChanceBase + critChancePerInstinct × instinct` inside `computeDamage`. `catchChance` never looks at it, so **bait success does not use Instinct at all** in the shipped reducer. At the level cap, the roster's Instinct spread of 40 base points buys about 2.9% of extra expected damage; the same points in Attack would buy several times that. Against a fixed 300-point budget, every point of Instinct is a point taken from something that works.
- **The cascade's FIRST half is reliable and its SECOND half is not.** Across every node × reading × shape, the top of the web reaches zero in a mean 69% of seeds; the complete beat — the tier below alive *and strictly rising* — peaks at **43%**.
- **`fixtures/overcatch-script.json` still reproduces its recorded trace exactly** at seed 12 — the apex gone, nothing hunting the tier below, and that tier off its floor.
- **The §7.4 predation bonus cannot influence a greedy attacker's move choice.** It is a function of (attacker line, defender line) only, so it multiplies every one of an attacker's moves by the same factor. An earlier version of the player model handicapped the player by hiding the food web from its scorer; the handicap changed nothing and was removed. Anyone reasoning about "learning the web" as a skill expression should know it currently is not one — it changes damage, never decisions.

### INFERRED

- **Most of the round-robin tail is the Instinct tail, and the rest is frailty.** Of the weak lines, Loamunder (ins 60), Sporantle (ins 65), Carricrow (ins 70) sit above the roster's median Instinct of 45; Skir (ins 40, hp 55, def 50) does not, and is instead among the frailest animals in the game. Against a fixed 300-point budget the honest reading is that the tail is not badly TYPED, it is badly SPENT. There are levers on both sides of the INV-3 line and §6.1 measures one of each: `damage.critChancePerInstinct` is a balance-file key and raising it moves the tail in the right direction, but not far enough to reach the band; reallocating `baseStats` in `data/species.json`, or giving the two 0-power role moves (Regrow, Scrounge) something to do, is content and would move it properly.
- **In a roster that is 59% decided, a type-multiplier retune reorders nothing.** Every `balance.types.*` multiplier applies to both sides of every matchup, so moving one rescales how fast a decided matchup resolves without changing who wins it. That rules the whole `types` group out as a fix for §3's tails, whatever it does to the feel of a fight.
- **The fixture's seed is doing more work than the fixture claims.** `fixtures/overcatch-script.json` says the beat "holds for a majority of low seeds". Re-run at `r4` with the fixture's own protocol shape over 128 seeds, the complete beat lands in 0% of them and the tier below co-extincts in 95%. Seed 12 is a winner, not a representative. The recorded demo is safe — it is a recording — but the claim that the beat is robust at that node is not supported.
- **A vertical-slice node is the better demo stage.** `r2` (Fenmaw over Reedrick + Mirefin, reading `limiting`, shape `hold`) produces the complete beat in 43% of seeds and refills the throne in 98% — against `r4`'s 13%. `r2` is walkable, which is where build-doc §1 says the video is shot. This closes the open risk in `docs/LANE-A-STATUS.md` §6.2, which recorded that reproducing the beat on the slice was "not demonstrated".

**Nothing below has been applied.** `data/balance.json` is byte-identical after this run; build-doc §6
INV-3 makes retuning a balance-file-only change, and this harness's job is to say what the numbers
are, not to make them flattering. Re-run against a candidate file with

1. **`encounter.ageWeights`: {"J":0.5,"A":0.4,"E":0.1} → {"J":0.25,"A":0.59,"E":0.16}.** Measured: forcing the shipped mix to all-Adult is worth +23.8 pp of gym win rate, so this is the single largest INV-3-legal lever on §2's misses. It also does real work for the fiction — the shipped J 0.5 means half of every team a player builds in an act is at the ×0.75 age multiplier while the leader fields Adults and Elders. The proposal moves half the juvenile weight out, three quarters of it to Adult. **Side effect to check before applying:** the same key drives the wild encounter mix, so juveniles — the cheapest catches, `catch.baseCatch.J` = 0.5 — become rarer.

2. **`encounter.levelByYear`: [6,12,18,24,30,36] → [8,14,20,26,30,38].** Raises the wild level in the acts whose gym missed low, which raises the level a team is caught at. §2.6 prices a level directly. This is the lever that moves the UNTRAINED model — the floor of the bracket — without touching `data/gyms.json`. **Caveat: bumping only the acts that missed leaves the ramp unevenly spaced (8 +6 +6 +6 +4 +8), which is not a shape anyone would design on purpose. Smooth it by hand before applying, or treat the array as evidence of how far off the ramp is rather than as a drop-in replacement.**

3. **`damage.critChancePerInstinct`: 0.0008 → 0.004.** Instinct correlates with peer win rate at r = -0.582 — **negatively** — and crit is the only thing `core/battle.ts` uses it for, so the price of a point of Instinct is the one balance-file lever on §3's tail. **Measured (§6.1): Mirefin -2.4 pp, Bramblore -0.3 pp, Duskstalk -4.9 pp, Gloomoth -1.1 pp, Nibbin -4.6 pp, Vulkindle +0.8 pp, Skir -1.1 pp, Loamunder +1.9 pp, Sporantle +6.9 pp, Carricrow +6.0 pp.** It moves the weakest lines up and the strongest down, which is the right direction — and it does not come close to closing the gap. The magnitude here is a multiple of the shipped value chosen to make the sensitivity visible, not a recommendation: treat it as the first point of a supervised sweep. Note the side effect — crit also gets louder for every OTHER line, and the player's own team is drafted from the same roster.

4. **No FURTHER delta is proposed for the round-robin tail, and that is the finding.** Skir 34%, Loamunder 27%, Sporantle 13%, Carricrow 8% are outside the band. Beyond the crit lever above, §3 shows why a balance retune cannot reach them: 59% of matchups are decided outright, so every `balance.types.*` multiplier applies to both sides and rescales the chart without reordering it. The remaining levers are `baseStats` in `data/species.json` and the role move pool in `data/moves.json` — **content**. That belongs in `docs/DEVIATIONS.md`, not in a balance retune.

5. **`cascade.mesoReleaseSeasons`: 4 → 6.** The strict "rising" clause is measured over this many seasons from the moment the apex hits zero. At the shipped breed rates (0, 0.03, 0.045, 0.05, 0.06, 0.09 per season) a population of one or two needs several seasons to add an individual, so the window and not the ecology may be what is failing the clause. **This is a claim-widening delta, and it must not be applied to make a red measurement green** — apply it only if the wider window is the honest reading of "released". The number lives in `balance.json` precisely so this decision is visible.

6. **A `breedRate` lift on the released tier would be the real fix, and it is NOT a balance delta.** Per-line breed rates live in `data/nodes/*.json` (build-doc §7.2 — the node file is the single source of truth). Raising the mesopredator breed rate at the demo node is the change that would turn "the throne is refilled" into "and it is visibly rising", and it is a content edit under `docs/DEVIATIONS.md`.

## 7. What this harness does not measure

Stated so that a green report is not mistaken for a finished game.

- **No lifecycle in the gym sim.** Aging, retirement, heirs and boons (build-doc §7.5) do not appear
  in §2. `docs/LANE-A-STATUS.md` §6.4 flags `retirement.heirLevelFactor` = 0.5 as producing a
  measured ~2.1× power drop in the same year as a level-24-plus gym. That is a real risk to the
  never-cut Legacy beat and this harness does not test it. A gym run seeded with a post-retirement
  party is the obvious next mode.
- **No trainer battles, no rival, no wild-battle attrition** — the gym is measured as a single
  encounter against a full-health party, which is the most favourable framing for the player.
- **No item economy.** The salve allowance is one per party member and is not paid for; the shop
  prices in `balance.economy` are never exercised.
- **Mode (c) does not simulate fights** (see §4.1 for why that is sound), so it cannot detect a
  balance problem that only shows up when a player has to *win* to keep catching.
- **The round-robin is 1v1 at one level.** Switching, party composition and the level curve are out
  of scope for it by construction, and 0 battles stalled at `balance.harness.maxBattleTurns` = 200 and are counted as draws.
- **Sampling error.** 500 seeds put a 1-sigma band of roughly ±2.2 pp on each gym win rate.
  **Sable (-0.2 pp) is inside it and should be read as "on the edge of its band", not as a miss.** The over-catch rates in §4.3 are on 128 seeds
  (±4.4 pp), which is wide enough that two rows differing by a few points are not distinguishable.

## 8. Where this harness departs from build-doc §10.5, and why

Never a silent change. Each of these is a decision the spec left open or a place the spec's
literal wording could not be satisfied as written.

1. **Mode (c) resolves catches with the §7.4 formula instead of simulating the fight.** §10.5(c) says
   "6 years of scripted catching against the sim" without saying whether the battles run. They are
   skipped, and the justification is a deduction rather than a budget: Hard Req 8 makes KO a faint,
   so no battle can move a population by one individual, and the catch is the only channel from play
   into the ecology (§7.3). §4.1 states this in the report itself; §7 records what it costs.
2. **Two level models, not one.** §10.5(a) says "a party at a level appropriate to that act" and does
   not say what that level is. Choosing one silently would decide the answer; both are reported.
3. **Two prey readings and two protocol shapes in the over-catch sweep.** §10.2 says "its primary
   prey" (singular); DEVIATIONS A2-D10 already records that the set reading is what actually starves
   a predator. The fixture's own recorded protocol is a third thing again — a fixed effort per
   season, not a hold. All four combinations are measured because they do not agree.
4. **"a new apex risen" is measured structurally, not by role name.** A node whose top predator is a
   Mesopredator (the Meadow, the Vale) has no Apex at all, so the sweep asks who sits at the top of
   the LIVE derived web rather than who carries a label. No species id and no role name is written
   anywhere under `tools/harness/` (G6/§8.3); the gates over `tools/` enforce it.
5. **The round-robin is flagged on two readings.** §10.5(b) says "every line vs every line", which
   makes every ordinary creature fight three sign-chain-gated legendaries. Both the literal aggregate
   and the peers-only aggregate are reported; the Ancients are called out rather than counted as
   defects.
6. **The harness's own sampling budget is not in `balance.json`.** Seeds per mode, drafts per party
   and the report path are measurement parameters, not gameplay constants, and `balance.json` is
   gated so that every leaf must survive `zBalance` (`balance_keys_survive_validation`) —
   `core/schemas.ts` belongs to another lane. They live in `tools/harness/params.ts` with a CLI.
   Everything §10.5 states as a CLAIM — the 500 seeds, the six target bands, the 35/65 flags, the
   turn cap, the reference over-catching player — is read from `balance.harness` (G11).
