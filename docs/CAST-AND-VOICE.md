# TROPHIC — CAST AND VOICE

Canonical for **what a line must do** and **who speaks it**. Content facts — species, moves, types, towns, gym rosters — come from `docs/content-bible.md`; systems, schemas and formulas from `docs/build-doc.md`. Where this document disagrees with either, they win and this one is corrected.

Two build-doc §16 guardrails govern every line in the corpus, quoted because they are the two a prose pass actually breaks:

> **G2** Do not invent species, moves, types, statuses, towns, place names, organisation names, or roster changes — bible values verbatim; a discrepancy is raised for Hank, never fixed in passing.

> **G7** Do not write moralizing copy about catching anywhere. No line may tell the player they did wrong, imply guilt, or deliver a lesson. Populations are reported the way a working field biologist reports them — plainly, as counts, with no reverence for the notebook they are written in.

The corpus is `data/dialogue.json` — 214 entries, 509 lines, 63 speakers (the 62 cast below plus `narration`).

# 1. THE STANDARD

## The one question

> **Would a person say this out loud, in this situation, at this length?**

Real speech is baggier than written prose. It repeats itself, it names things twice, it keeps the small connecting words a writer would cut. Plain and literal beats compressed and clever every time.

## Linearity — the readability rule

> **One idea to a sentence. Say it in the order it happens. When the subject changes, start a new sentence.**

A reader loses a sentence when it makes them hold something unresolved. Three ways to do that, all independent of length:

1. **Subject switching.** `A does X, and B is Y, so C does Z` becomes three sentences. Two subjects joined is ordinary speech and is fine. **Three is a chain, and the chain is the defect** — not the word count that usually comes with it.
2. **Front-loading.** Main clause first: *"The bark is dead where the trunk has gone flat"*, not the reverse. A short fronted clause is fine.
3. **Self-interruption.** Subject, then verb. A relative clause wedged between them makes the reader hold the subject open.

Straightening syntax is not flattening voice: the grievance, the expertise and the opinion all stay, and every number, species, place, price and mechanic carries over exactly (G2).

## The five checks

- **F1 — REFERENTS.** Every `it`, `that`, `them`, `one`, `hers` resolves inside the same sentence or the one before it. If a reader has to reconstruct the referent, the line is broken.
- **F2 — LITERAL TRUTH.** Every sentence is literally true of its literal subject. A place is not three weeks old; the count of it is. A net is not a record. A metaphor that will not resolve is out.
- **F3 — NO STRUCTURAL CLEVERNESS IN THE LAST SENTENCE OF A BEAT.** If the closing sentence summarises, balances, reverses, or would look good printed on a poster, delete it. A beat ends on a fact, a request, a complaint, a piece of business, or an unfinished thought. **The ban is on the POSITION, not on a phrase list** — a freshly-invented epigram in the final slot is the same defect as a recycled one. **There is no aphorism budget and there are no licences**; rationing a move that reads as machine-written only distributes it.
- **THE JOB TEST.** Does the line come out of this person's actual work, in this person's actual vocabulary? A netmender says *gauge* and *re-cut*; a buyer says *clause* and *schedule*; a shafthand says *roster* and *shift*. If two characters in different trades could have reached for the same image, one is borrowing and it is not their image to borrow.
- **THE MENTOR TEST.** Second-person instruction belongs to **Maren, Gerta, Bram, Orrin** and the explicit shop-tutorial lines, and only about a method that character is personally responsible for. **Everyone else is allowed to not care that the player exists.** The dockhand is landing a barge; the innkeeper wants the room paid for; the child is showing off. None of them is helping you develop.

## Two quotas and a cap

- **THE COMPETENCE QUOTAS.** Real crowds contain a person who is boring, a person who is wrong, a person who repeats himself inside one conversation, and a person who does not answer the question he was asked. **All four are filled per settlement** (§2's grid). Corpus-wide, **at least eight speakers state something another speaker contradicts.** If every speaker is sharp, informative and quietly wise, the town is staffed rather than populated.
- **THE SMALL-TALK QUOTA.** Every ambient speaker gets **at least one line of their three carrying no ecological information whatsoever** — money, weather, a bad back, a neighbour, a delivery, a grievance about a rota. Ecology sits in the other two. A line with no ecological payload is a person being a person and needs no further warrant.
- **THE HARD CAP.** **No construction may be shared by more than two speakers in the whole game.** Not two per town — two in the corpus. If a third character reaches for a shape a second character already owns, the third loses it. This governs *shapes*, not sentences: two people independently saying "Morning." is not a shared construction; five speakers running one rhetorical frame is.

## How counting is talked about

**The word is SURVEY. "Ledger" is gone** — from the screen, from prose, from entry ids, flags, scene keys, filenames and tests. The scene is `SurveyScene.ts`; its test is `survey-view.test.ts`. Survey does not simply take ledger's place in a sentence, though, because a one-for-one swap keeps the halo and changes only the noun:

> **A survey is a thing you DO. The paper is a thing you CARRY.** You *walk* a survey. You're *due* on the west scarp. You *run* a transect. What you carry is **the survey book**, the board, the sheet, the page, the log, my column, a card. **Nobody in Fallowmere has "the survey" as an object, and nobody has "the ledger" at all.**

1. **EVERY NUMBER HAS AN OWNER.** Nobody says "the count is nine." They say *"I've got it at nine"*, *"the fishery has it at twelve"*, *"that's Maren's figure, not mine"*. A figure with no provenance is the tell of the machine voice.
2. **EVERY NUMBER HAS AN AGE, AND MOST ARE STALE.** The default state of the survey book is BEHIND. *"That's last spring's."* *"Three weeks out of date north of the gate, and that's my fault."*
3. **METHOD IS ARGUABLE, AND PEOPLE ARGUE IT.** Transect hour, double-counting, sample size, whose turn the west scarp was. **This is where the drama of counting lives** — not in reverence, in methodology disputes between people who each privately think the other is sloppy. It is also how Ives and Orrin, and Maren and Sel, express affection.
4. **COUNTING IS A CHORE WITH FRICTION.** Wet pages. A lost pencil. A crooked column. Handwriting nobody can read. A transect skipped because it rained and written up anyway.
5. **NOBODY SAYS IT MATTERS.** The moment a character asserts the importance of counting, the line is wrong. Its importance is shown by people being **paid off it**, being **late with it**, and **fighting about it**. The survey may be LATE, WRONG, MINE or DUE. Never honest, true, real, sacred, or the only thing that doesn't lie. **Corollary, checkable:** if the record is the *subject* of a sentence, the predicate is mechanical. It is behind. It is wet. It is on the board. It says nine. Never: it records, it does not care, it will read differently either way.

**Every trade has its own instrument and its own word for it** — the strongest single anti-chorus device in the corpus, because it means no two people call the same thing by the same name. **Maren** the survey, the sheet, the board, coverage · **Wick** intake, returns, grade, the column · **Greaves** the inventory, exposure, the audit · **Hetty** my board, chalk, damp, a crooked column · **Gerta** my log, one reading a shift · **Ives** the census, an identity, a plate · **Orrin** the margin, the margin book, the hand · **Sel** pole depths, my marks, my grandfather's card · **Tolm** the headcount, walked at the turn · **Corbett** the contract figure, the clause · **Parnel** the mesh, the gauge · **Whit** lamps out, lamps lit · **Idony** the lamp bills · **Snell** what his hands tell him · **Bram** what the floor is doing · **Odo** the water · **Pinder** the chart, the rate.

**At least four ambient speakers are BORED by counting** — a world where everybody respects the survey is a world where the survey means nothing. Mandatory: **Mott** ("I've not got a number. I've got a post"), **Parnel** ("I cut the mesh to what comes up in it, and I'd not call that counting"), **Nunn** ("Ask me about the roster instead"), **Hesk** ("I don't ask and they don't say").

**Where G7 could break.** Stripping the reverence removes praise as well as blame, and the risk it introduces is that irreverence curdles into sarcasm aimed at the player. Characters may be short with their data and short with each other. **No character is ever short with the player's choices.** The epilogues stay numeric. The G7 scan cannot detect tone, so the control is a line-by-line read by someone who did not write the line.

## Line length — a distribution, not a target

Sentence length is not measured (§3). **Line** length has a shape, because the dialogue box is 74 columns × 3 rows — about 222 characters a page — and a long line becomes advance-presses mid-clause.

- **Median 130–170** across the corpus, and **at least 60 of ~500 lines under 80 characters**. A corpus where nothing is short has no emphasis, because emphasis is contrast.
- **Every speaker with three or more lines has at least one line under 90 characters** (`G1-NO-SHORT`). **Hard ceiling 300**; a line over 220 needs a reason.
- **Vary WITHIN a beat.** A three-line beat running 260 / 250 / 270 is one paragraph chopped in three. Run it 210 / 60 / 180. The short line is where the emphasis lives.
- **Who is allowed to be long:** Maren explaining method, Sel telling a story, Greaves stating a position, Orrin reading an archive, and the two elders whose whole slot is length — Judd meandering and Dunstan holding forth. **Not** children, innkeepers, vendors, a dockhand landing a barge, or anyone doing a job with their hands while they talk — those people speak in fragments and get interrupted by the work.

# 2. THE CAST

## Naming register

Three tiers, set by the work and not by money. **HANDS** — field, dock, shaft, road, crew, boat: one hard syllable, or two with a closed ending. **SKILLED / SALARIED** — innkeepers, stallholders, winders, lampwrights, shepherds, elders: a softer two syllables, often a saint's or grandmother's name. **INSTITUTIONAL** — anyone speaking for a contract or a company is **surname alone**, exactly as Greaves is; **every Cullbrand employee in every town is surname-only**, so the player hears "company" before the character says a word. **CHILDREN** are diminutives, and are the only vowel-final or pet-form names in the cast.

**Any name added later must pass both screens before it ships.** *(1) Data collision.* No exact match against any species form, dex id, line id, node id, node display name, town, gym name, nickname, trait, move or item; **no shared first three letters** with any of those tokens, with another cast name, or with a protected canon name; and it must not appear as a lowercase ordinary word in the corpus. Rejected classes, with the reasons: fish words (a dockhand named after a fish reads as a species), `Cul-` heads (Cullbrand), common nouns pressed into service as names, pop-culture-adjacent names in the child tier. *(2) Pokémon screen (G15).* Match against PokéAPI species / moves / abilities / items / forms and Bulbapedia's full-text index. **Rename** if the Pokémon character has their own page or recurs across several appearances; **keep** if it is a single anonymous row in a Trainer table. Screening 54 ordinary short English given names turned up a **~28% exact strike rate** on Trainer/character hits — plain human names are not safe by default in this space.

## The three principals

They carry the argument of the game, and what keeps them from sounding like one person having it with himself is that **they are arguing about different things in different professional languages, and only one of them thinks it is an argument at all.** Maren argues about **method** — what was measured, by whom, how recently. Greaves is not arguing; he is **quoting terms**, and assumes the terms settle it. Wick is arguing about **whether he can afford to change his mind**, and loses that argument to himself over six acts.

**`maren` — Warden Maren.** Late fifties, the only person in Fallowmere paid to count. She is **not the voice of the region**: she is one underfunded person with a board, a pencil and more ground than she can walk, and she says so as a practical complaint, not as humility. Coverage is her daily problem — Meadow Reach she walks often because it is next door, Brackmarsh is three weeks old and the three weeks were wet, the steppe she has not been up since the thaw and Gerta does it for her. She wants the player's Reach numbers because she cannot be in two places, which is a reason and not a rite. She separates what she measured from what she suspects, out loud, every time. **Tic:** the age and provenance of a figure arrive before the figure. **Opinions she has and states:** Gerta's log is better data than anything Maren owns; Hetty's board is homemade and she trusts it because she has watched Hetty take a count; half of Orrin's margin is in handwriting nobody can read; Sel is right about the water table for the wrong reason and she finds that irritating; Corbett's contract figure is not a count and she would not price a boot off it. **Never** tells the player what to do, treats the survey as anything but a board and a pencil usually out of date, or quotes a number she cannot source. **Across the acts:** 1 recruiting cheap labour and honest about it · 2 behind · 3 behind and naming which nodes · 4 the steppe number is one she has never had cause to write, said flatly, once · 5 in the archive because Orrin has data older than hers · 6 at the rim with a board because that is where she works.

**`wick` — Wick.** Seventeen, second-year Cullbrand apprentice, quick and openly ambitious. He thinks in **documents** and cites them the way other people cite feelings; he wants to be impressive and is irritated that he wants it. Short declaratives that get shorter as he loses an argument. **Tic:** answers anything emotional with a figure, then names a specific piece of paperwork and offers to show it. His pay grade rises across the six acts and he mentions it every time, which is characterising and, by act 6, quietly sad. He never moralises in either direction, never reaches for a nature metaphor, and **never concedes with a maxim — he concedes with a number.**

| Act | Position |
|---|---|
| 1 | Company vocabulary worn proudly. He names his own grade and pay because he thinks that IS the argument. |
| 2 | **He is wrong, out loud, on the record**: he prices Brackwater's fishery off the **Fenmaw** count; it is indexed to **Mirefin/channel**. **Corbett corrects him flatly in front of the player**; Wick concedes instantly, because conceding is cheap — and then quietly stops quoting figures he has not checked himself. |
| 3 | Bowerdeep: nobody is outside the trade. He is right and it costs him nothing to be right. He notices the player is not paid, cannot work out why they are still here, and says so. |
| 4 | The steppe. **He worked it** — two seasons, west scarp, took the bonus. He does not defend it and does not apologise; he states his part and reaches for a schedule word. Shortest lines of his part. |
| 5 | He repeats Greaves' argument accurately, admits he has never found the one that beats it, and asks the player for the counter-argument. **The first thing he has ever asked for.** |
| 6 | He is not going in. **The hinge is arithmetic, not conscience** — he learned on the steppe what the column looks like after the last draw, and there is nothing under it. He wants to be beaten so he can say the argument is his. |

**`greaves` — Magnate Greaves.** Sixty-odd, expensively educated, immensely wealthy, entirely courteous, and the only person in the game with a legal department. He addresses the player as a counterparty who has turned up at the wrong meeting. **A magnate does not compress; a magnate qualifies:** complete periodic sentences with subordinate clauses, **no contractions ever**, delivered at a speed that assumes nobody will interrupt. **He is never the agent** — the contract closed, the board took a view, the lawyers consider the distinction load-bearing, the schedule was met early; *he* appears only where money does, and he says *we* for the Company and *I* for the money. Every noun he touches becomes a class of asset within a clause or two, **and the conversion is done by a clause, not by an epigram** ("A keystone is an input" is the philosopher's version). He never dignifies Maren's book by comparing it to his: hers is **counting**, a verb; his is **the inventory**, a noun with a department behind it. **Never** insults the player, expresses appetite or cruelty, raises his voice, uses slang or a metaphor, speaks a sentence under six words (that register is Wick's), or explains himself twice.

## Town registers

- **HEARTHOLLOW** — *homely, agricultural, English.* The baseline: plain declaratives, domestic vocabulary, almost nobody volunteering anything to a stranger unasked. People are known by their households, so **given names**, used by the whole town. Nobody here has read a contract; nobody uses a technical word except Tamsin, who uses too many and defines them all. Time is measured in harvests and in "before they moved the gate". The town's argument is about **fences and gates** — Mott, Rask, Judd and Tolm are in a slow disagreement about who moved what and when.
- **BRACKWATER** — *wet, contractual, North-Sea trade port.* The town where **money is a document**: every wage moves when Corbett signs, so contract language runs through mouths that have never read one — levy, clause, the season, the price. ## The four props

**The props are objects. They are not a narrator, and they are certainly not a wise one.** A prop reading is a **field observation written by somebody who came to record the object, not to be moved by it.** Present tense, two to four short declaratives, everything verifiable by a hand or an eye standing where the player is standing. Whatever is strange **arrives as a measurement that does not fit, and is left uninterpreted.** Universal bans: no `as if`; no `someone`/`whoever` as a mystery device; no second person — the player is never addressed and never given equipment they do not own; no mood adjectives; no inference of intent; no closing line of portent. No two props share a closing rhythm and no prop repeats its own two readings running. B8 survives here and only here, once per prop.

- **`prop-antler-stone` — Antler Standing-Stone.** Fixed order every time: material and size · orientation and setting · weathering · what is carved. One quantity per beat is out of place and is not explained — the groove has two depths; the stone is warm where the grass banks against it; the herd is six figures on this one as on the last. **Never** infers intent ("Whoever kept these stones kept them long enough to need to repair them" is the exact move to cut). **This speaker narrates no cutscene** — the Vellsire arrival belongs to `narration`, because `DialogueScene` draws the name plate from the speaker with no cutscene exemption.
- **`prop-sunken-idol` — Sunken Idol.** The same discipline with water as the variable: what is visible, what is obscured, and exactly where certainty stops. Shorter and more provisional than the antler stones. Each beat pairs a fact about the carving with a fact about the water directly beneath it **and does not connect them**. **Never** "stares" or any cousin; never cites Sel's opinion as corroboration. **The eyelessness is held to `chain.undermere.step3`** — before that the idol reports only that the face will not come clear through the water. It is Sel's reveal.
- **`prop-shell-kiln-shrine` — Shell-Kiln Shrine.** Material forensics on something miners have worked around for generations. The mine's own words are admissible — plates, joins, the reach, the shaft head — and human wear is admissible evidence because it is measurable. Each beat **identifies the material by getting it wrong first and then correcting**, which is how a person actually inspects a thing. **Never** temple vocabulary: it is a shrine because miners set candles in it, not because anything sanctified it. Warmth appears only as a measurement.
- **`prop-sign-card` — Sign Card.** Not narration. A board, and signage **accumulates in layers**: an administrative heading in capitals, one or two lines of instruction, a fee or a rule, standard abbreviations — then later hands in pencil that correct, answer, amend or argue with the printed text above them. The official half never uses the first person; the annotations always do and are never complete formal sentences. **The last hand on a board is usually annoyed.** **Never** an aphorism; never editorialises about counting; never the word ledger — the boards say SURVEY, in the flat voice of a posted regulation.

# 3. WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT REQUIRED

One rule governs this section: **a bar every line must clear is a bar every line will be bent to clear.** Each item below was withdrawn because it was being read as a quota and was manufacturing the uniformity the standard exists to prevent. Do not reinstate one.

- **Attributability is scoped, not universal.** The four-line test — strip the name plate, set the line beside three others from the same town, and a reader who knows the cast can say which is which — **applies only to a line doing characterisation work**: a beat where someone reveals a position, a grievance, an expertise, or a relationship. **It does not apply to functional speech.** Greetings, prices, directions, confirmations and refusals are finished lines as they are. *"Morning." · "It's over there." · "Two coppers."* could each have come out of any of five mouths, and that is correct, because naming a price is any-of-five-mouths work. **A line does not get better by being made distinctive.** Ordinary functional speech is not a failure of characterisation; it is what most speech is.
- **The specificity floor is scoped the same way.** "At least one thing only this speaker would know" is a demand on characterisation lines. A price line carries a price.
- **Length is not a defect.** Hank, verbatim: *"I'M OK WITH LONG SENTENCES IF THEY ARE UNDERSTANDABLE."* A long sentence that runs straight from its subject to its end is correct and needs no repair. **What is measured is linearity** — subject-switching, front-loading, self-interruption — **never word count.** Rewriting a long sentence that already reads straight is out of scope, at any length. No check counts sentence length as a defect in either direction, and the corpus-level check has a floor and no ceiling: it fails a corpus for having *too few* long sentences.
- **There is no vocabulary bar.** No word list, no register floor, no requirement that a line reach for the trade's own noun. The disease was never word choice; it was compression.
- **There is no length floor on a line.** Short is not broken. *"The post's down again"* is short and fine; *"I've not got a number"* is broken because it is a punchline, not because it is short.
- **`G13-NOUN-CARRIED`'s corpus-wide share is diagnostic only and is NOT to be driven down.** Roughly half the lines in the corpus naming a proper noun or a trade object is what a working population of tradespeople in a named region sounds like; ordinary speech names ordinary things. **The only actionable reading is per speaker**, at the top of the list: a speaker whose *every* line leans on a noun has props rather than a voice, and is worth a look.
- **Withdrawing a bar does not commission a pass.** No shipped line is to be rewritten on the strength of this section.

# 4. THE INSTRUCTION BAR

> **A beat that teaches the player how to play must leave a first-time reader able to say what to do, how to do it, and what happens as a result. If the reader cannot, the beat has failed — no matter how well it reads or how well it is voiced.**

This is the one place in the corpus where **being understood outranks being in character.**

**Which beats it binds — 29 of 214.** `maren.opening`, the three starter beats, `maren.tutorial.snare`, `maren.tutorial.survey`, `maren.tutorial.release`, `maren.retirement`, the ten `*.quest.*.offer` beats, the five `*.rest` and five `*.shop` service beats, and `sel.service.ferry`. **A beat is bound by this rule if the player has no other source for the thing it says.**

**The test is a rig, not an opinion.**

> Give the beat's text — and nothing else: no code, no design document, no glossary, no other beat — to a reader who has never seen this game. Ask three questions. *What am I supposed to do? How do I do it? What happens when I do?* Compare the three answers against what the code actually does.

A mismatch is a defect in the beat. **The reader is never wrong.** If a reader concludes bait is optional when the code makes it a multiplier, the line is unclear and the repair is the line. The rig's one hard requirement: **the reader must not be able to see the answer.** A reader with repo access reconstructs the mechanic from the code and reports that the beat was clear, and that result measures nothing.

**What this does not license.** It **does not make anyone a tutor** — the mentor test stands, nobody explains the game to the player as a game, and Maren explains her job because the job *is* the mechanic. It **does not license system vocabulary**: "node", "count", "base rate", "coefficient", "age class" stay out of every mouth, and an instruction that needs a system word is an instruction pointed at the wrong noun — name the thing in the world, not the field in the save file. It **does not license moralising**: G7 stands over every word of this section, and a tutorial beat explaining that catching lowers a number is reporting a number — the moment it implies the player ought to feel something about that number, the pass has failed. It **does not extend to flavour**: an ambient line by a logger is not an instruction.

# 5. ENFORCEMENT

`python3 tools/prose/prose-lint.py data/dialogue.json` · `--selftest` runs the controls (every check has a positive and a negative one). Exit 0 clean, 1 flags, **2 = the linter itself is broken**. **Its `G`-codes are its own numbering and are unrelated to the build-doc guardrails** — `G2-TENURE` is not guardrail G2.

**Per-beat checks** (word-level): `F3-EPIGRAM` a structural closer in the last sentence · `F2-CATEGORY` a property of the record predicated of the thing · `B1-ANTITHESIS` X-not-Y · `F1-DANGLING` a beat opening on a pronoun with no referent · `F2-EQUATION` a metaphor compressed into a false equation · `G1-NO-SHORT` a speaker with no short beats at all · `G2-TENURE` a tenure-credential opener · `G3-VINDICATION` the "I said it and nobody listened" stance · `R1-SUBJECT-SWITCH` · `R2-FRONTLOAD` · `R3-INTERRUPT`.

**Corpus checks** (shape-level): `G8-ASSERT-CHOP` · `G9-SHAPE-SIG` two closing sentences sharing an abstract shape · `B1-DISTRIBUTED` the negation-correction split across a full stop or comma · `G11-CLOSER-SHAPE` beats closing on the same move · `G12-SILHOUETTE` the two-line beat with one fixed silhouette · `G13-NOUN-CARRIED` **(diagnostic, never a failure — see §3)** · `R4-NOT-TELEGRAMS`, a floor with no ceiling whose controls include a corpus of long sentences that must pass and a corpus of short ones that must fail, so driving length down turns the linter red. The linter's shared-construction cap is 2, matching §1.

**What the linter cannot check.** Sense, comprehension and tone. **No regex reads for comprehension** — §4 is checked by the reader rig and by nothing else, and **a green linter is not evidence that an instruction landed**, or that a line means anything, or that G7's tone held. The gate is an independent read-aloud pass by someone who did not write the line, whose only question is *does every sentence parse, and does every word point at something?* The linter is the floor beneath that.

**Test gates any corpus edit must respect** (`tests/unit/dialogue.test.ts`):

- **Line budget.** `LINE_COUNT` 400–560, currently 509, and `raw._counts.lines` is asserted against the file — update it in the same commit as any count change. Ambient: min 3, **median exactly 3**. Generic: min 3. Named: min 6, median 8–16.
- **There is no per-speaker ceiling, and one must not be reintroduced.** Hank: *"remove the ceiling. I don't care about how much dialogue a character has."* The floors and the medians stay, because they describe a DISTRIBUTION — a corpus where every speaker says the same amount reads as a form letter, and one where a named speaker says almost nothing reads as a stub. A cap is a different claim, it was the author's and not his, and it is gone. Only the corpus-level `LINE_COUNT` band bounds total volume.
- **Name allow-lists.** `BIBLE_NAMES` (cap 8), `COINED_NPC_NAMES` (cap 8), `CAST_NAMES` (cap 48). The assertion is **one-way**: every token in every `speakers[].name` must resolve into the union. **Never source a list from `raw.speakers`** — the list gates the prose; the prose never gates the list, or the file certifies its own inventions.
- **Content-truth maps.** `DIET_CLAIMS`, `FLORA_CLAIMS`, `DIET_LINE_REVIEW` (which *counts* predation-verb lines per entry), `COUNT_CLAIM_REVIEW`, `CALENDAR_YEAR_CLAIMS` and `ACT_GATED_YEAR_CLAIMS` are hand-maintained registers keyed by entry id, asserted as exact set equality. Re-audit every rewritten entry against all six and update them in the same commit. `COUNT_CLAIM_REVIEW`'s regex fires on ordinary English, not just on claims — *"three left"*, *"one of them"*, *"down to four"* all turn an entry into a count entry that must be classified `live` or `world`. Run it over new lines before handing them over.
- **The G7 scan fires on tokens, not sentiment**, and its header states they are banned **even inside a negating sentence**: blame / blamed / blaming / to blame, `hold ~ against`, your fault, deserve, lesson, teach you, moral, conscience, guilt, shame, sin, cruel, greed, selfish, evil, repent, "should not have caught", "know when to", "no right to". Any voice tic phrased as "blames X" must be re-expressed. Its `KNOWN_GOOD` negative controls must be lines that actually survive in the shipped corpus, or the controls stop testing anything while still passing.
- **Reachability.** `tests/gates/reachability.test.ts` and `tests/gates/quest-layer.test.ts` — a new entry is not authored until something outside the test produces its precondition and a selector in `game/` can reach its kind.
